# Golden file: the component menu, rendered for the shipped catalog.
#
# Two implementations render this text — _catalog_menu() in
# worker/lusora_worker/agents/planner.py and componentMenu() in
# platform/src/lib/catalog.ts — and both assert against this file. It is the
# only robust way to keep two languages in one voice: a menu that drifts is
# discovered here, in CI, rather than in a prompt six weeks later.
#
# Regenerate with:
#   cd worker && uv run python -m lusora_worker.agents.planner --write-menu-fixture
#
# A diff here means the menu every planner call carries has changed. That is
# allowed; it is not allowed to happen by accident.
=== selection ===
- AnimatedCounter (anchor types: number/percentage; holds ~4s)
  when to use: the narration lands a single figure worth dwelling on — a count, a total, a percentage ('29,000 tanks', '70% of factories')
  when NOT to use: two or more values being compared (ComparisonSplit for two, BarChart for three or more); a figure that should sit in the corner while the shot continues (StatTag); a position in a ranking (RankLabel)
- ArchivalFrame (anchor types: none — pure text allowed; holds ~5s)
  when to use: presenting the shot itself as archival footage — grain, gate flicker and an optional slate line over a period clip
  when NOT to use: a still photograph that should read as an exhibit with a caption (FramedExhibit); a paper document (DocumentCard); any shot where the underlying footage should stay clean
- BarChart (anchor types: comparison; holds ~5.5s)
  when to use: the narration compares three to seven named quantities of the same kind ('Germany 12,000; the Soviets 29,000; the Americans 49,000')
  when NOT to use: exactly two values, where the split reads better (ComparisonSplit); one value (AnimatedCounter); a quantity changing over time (LineChart); dated events (Timeline)
- BulletList (anchor types: none — pure text allowed; holds ~6s)
  when to use: the narration enumerates two to five short parallel claims, reasons or consequences in sequence
  when NOT to use: stages of a process that happen in order (StepFlow); dated events (Timeline); label/value facts about one subject (FactSheet); a single claim (HammerStatement)
- CalloutArrow (anchor types: none — pure text allowed; holds ~3.5s)
  when to use: pointing at something already visible in the footage and naming it ('this is the breach in the hull')
  when NOT to use: the shot has nothing specific to point at — use a card instead (FactCard); locating a place on a map (SatelliteLocate); marking words in quoted text (HighlightedPassage)
- Candlestick (anchor types: none — pure text allowed; holds ~6.5s)
  when to use: a price or index moving over an ordered sequence where the RANGE within each period matters — how far it swung, not just where it closed
  when NOT to use: a quantity where only the level matters at each point (LineChart); values at one moment (BarChart); how a single total was built up or eroded (WaterfallChart); one figure and its change (MetricGrid)
- ChapterCard (anchor types: none — pure text allowed; holds ~4s)
  when to use: a full-screen act break between sections of the video — numbered chapter, title, optional subtitle
  when NOT to use: the opening title of the video or a stylised statement (KineticTitle); a heading that should not stop the narration; a person's name (NamePlate)
- ComparisonSplit (anchor types: comparison; holds ~5s)
  when to use: the narration sets exactly two quantities of the same kind against each other and the contrast is the point ('eleven ships lost against two')
  when NOT to use: three or more values (BarChart); one value (AnimatedCounter); two things compared qualitatively rather than numerically (FactSheet)
- DataTable (anchor types: none — pure text allowed; holds ~6s)
  when to use: several subjects compared across several attributes — three tanks by weight, gun and crew; four countries by year. Two to four columns, two to six rows, with a header
  when NOT to use: label/value rows about ONE subject, or a ranking of one kind of thing (FactSheet, with `numbered` for the ranking); values whose SIZES are the comparison rather than their exact figures (BarChart); two subjects on one attribute (ComparisonSplit); dated events (Timeline)
- DateStamp (anchor types: date; holds ~3.5s)
  when to use: establishing when (and optionally where) a shot happens, as a corner slug the narration runs underneath
  when NOT to use: several dates in sequence (Timeline); a date that is itself the subject of the beat (AnimatedCounter for a year figure); locating a place (SatelliteLocate)
- DefinitionCard (anchor types: none — pure text allowed; holds ~5s)
  when to use: the narration introduces a technical term or piece of jargon the viewer is unlikely to know, and defines it
  when NOT to use: a claim or statistic rather than a term (FactCard); a person's name and role (NamePlate); a quoted line (QuoteBlock)
- DocumentCard (anchor types: none — pure text allowed; holds ~6s)
  when to use: reproducing a written source as paper — an order, a telegram, a signed note — while the narration reads or discusses it
  when NOT to use: a spoken line (QuoteBlock); marking phrases inside a passage (HighlightedPassage); a photograph (FramedExhibit); a list of the video's own claims (BulletList)
- FactCard (anchor types: none — pure text allowed; holds ~5.5s)
  when to use: one claim that needs a sentence of explanation and, ideally, a source — the workhorse side panel
  when NOT to use: a bare number (AnimatedCounter or StatTag); a defined term (DefinitionCard); several parallel claims (BulletList); a blunt one-line statement (HammerStatement)
- FactSheet (anchor types: none — pure text allowed; holds ~6s)
  when to use: two to six label/value rows in one panel: a dossier on one subject (ship, aircraft, person, place), or with `numbered` a RANKING of the same kind of thing (the five deadliest, the ten biggest)
  when NOT to use: values compared across subjects, where the SIZES are the point (BarChart, or ComparisonSplit for two); one position in a ranking rather than the list of them (RankLabel); prose claims with no values (BulletList); dated events (Timeline)
- FramedExhibit (anchor types: none — pure text allowed; holds ~5s)
  when to use: presenting a photograph or artefact as an exhibit, with a caption and optional credit
  when NOT to use: treating the whole shot as archival footage (ArchivalFrame); a written document (DocumentCard); a caption over live footage with nothing to frame (FactCard)
- HammerStatement (anchor types: none — pure text allowed; holds ~3.5s)
  when to use: one blunt line that is the point of the section, filling the frame — a turn, a verdict, a reveal
  when NOT to use: a claim that needs explaining (FactCard); a section heading (ChapterCard); the video's title (KineticTitle); anything someone actually said (QuoteBlock)
- HeadlineStack (anchor types: none — pure text allowed; holds ~6.5s)
  when to use: two to five press headlines land in sequence to show that everyone reported it at once — the montage IS the argument
  when NOT to use: the video's own claims in its own voice (BulletList); one headline worth reading in full (FactCard, or DocumentCard for the page itself); something an individual posted (SocialPost); dated events on a rail (Timeline)
- HighlightedPassage (anchor types: quote; holds ~6s)
  when to use: a passage of quoted text where specific phrases are being picked apart — the marks land on the words as the narration reaches them
  when NOT to use: a quote presented whole with no phrase singled out (QuoteBlock); paper documents (DocumentCard); pointing at the footage (CalloutArrow)
- KineticTitle (anchor types: none — pure text allowed; holds ~3.5s)
  when to use: the video's opening title or a cold-open line, assembling word by word
  when NOT to use: a mid-video section break (ChapterCard); a claim being argued (HammerStatement); a name (NamePlate)
- LineChart (anchor types: none — pure text allowed; holds ~6.5s)
  when to use: a quantity changing across an ordered sequence — production by year, losses by month — for up to three series
  when NOT to use: values at one moment (BarChart); a single figure (AnimatedCounter); events rather than measurements (Timeline)
- MetricGrid (anchor types: none — pure text allowed; holds ~5s)
  when to use: two to six headline figures shown together as peers, each with how it moved — a results block, a quarter at a glance
  when NOT to use: label/value rows about one subject read top to bottom, where no figure dominates (FactSheet); ONE figure worth dwelling on (AnimatedCounter); several subjects across several attributes (DataTable); figures compared by size (BarChart)
- NamePlate (anchor types: name; holds ~4s)
  when to use: introducing a person by name while the narration continues — role and lifespan optional
  when NOT to use: a place (SatelliteLocate); a term (DefinitionCard); a section heading (ChapterCard); quoting what the person said (QuoteBlock)
- PieChart (anchor types: percentage/comparison; holds ~5s)
  when to use: the narration splits ONE total into its parts — 'a third of the budget went to the navy', 'two in five never returned'. Values are absolute; the component takes the share
  when NOT to use: quantities that are not parts of the same whole, where the sizes are what is compared (BarChart); two values set against each other (ComparisonSplit); one share on its own, with no remainder worth drawing (AnimatedCounter with a '%' suffix, or StatTag); a share changing over time (LineChart)
- PortraitPlates (anchor types: name; holds ~5s)
  when to use: presenting one or two people or places as framed pictures — a portrait on a mat with the name set beside it, or a pair of plates side by side under one kicker
  when NOT to use: one photograph the words are ABOUT, with a sentence of caption under it (FramedExhibit); naming somebody while their footage keeps running, with no picture of your own (NamePlate); treating the running shot itself as archival (ArchivalFrame)
- QuoteBlock (anchor types: quote; holds ~6s)
  when to use: a line someone actually said or wrote, presented whole with attribution
  when NOT to use: specific phrases inside the quote are being dissected (HighlightedPassage); the source is a document being shown as paper (DocumentCard); the video's own assertion (HammerStatement)
- RankLabel (anchor types: number; holds ~4s)
  when to use: a countdown or ranked list, marking which entry this is ('#3 of 20')
  when NOT to use: any number that is not a position in a ranking (AnimatedCounter); the ranked items shown together (BarChart); ordered stages of a process (StepFlow)
- RegionHighlight (anchor types: none — pure text allowed; holds ~6s)
  when to use: a territory whose EXTENT matters — occupied ground, an empire, a front — outlined and filled on a map
  when NOT to use: a single point on the map (SatelliteLocate); a journey between points (RouteMap); a place mentioned in passing. Requires a border polygon supplied by hand — nothing derives one, so this is placed in the editor, never chosen by the planner
- RouteMap (anchor types: place; holds ~6.5s)
  when to use: a journey or advance between two to six named places, drawn stop by stop
  when NOT to use: one place (SatelliteLocate); an area rather than a path (RegionHighlight); a sequence of events with no geography (Timeline); places the gazetteer does not know
- SatelliteLocate (anchor types: place; holds ~5s)
  when to use: the narration names a specific place and the viewer needs to know where it is — a crosshair lands on the coordinate
  when NOT to use: a territory's extent (RegionHighlight); a route between places (RouteMap); places mentioned in passing that need no locating; a place already established in the same section
- SocialPost (anchor types: quote; holds ~5.5s)
  when to use: the video quotes something somebody posted online and the SOURCE is part of the claim — a YouTube comment, a tweet, a Reddit card. `platform` picks the chrome
  when NOT to use: a quotation from a book, a speech or an interview, where the words matter and the medium does not (QuoteBlock); a page rather than a post (WebPageFrame); a press headline (HeadlineStack); naming the person without showing what they wrote (NamePlate)
- StatTag (anchor types: number/percentage; holds ~4s)
  when to use: a figure that should sit quietly in a corner while the footage carries the shot — a rate, a total, a share
  when NOT to use: the number is the point of the beat and deserves the frame (AnimatedCounter); several numbers (BarChart or FactSheet); a rank (RankLabel)
- StepFlow (anchor types: none — pure text allowed; holds ~6s)
  when to use: two to five stages of a process in order, where each step leads to the next — how a thing was built, decided or executed
  when NOT to use: unordered parallel claims (BulletList); events fixed to dates (Timeline); a comparison (BarChart)
- TextBanner (anchor types: none — pure text allowed; holds ~4s)
  when to use: any line worth stating flat across the shot while the footage keeps running — what we are looking at, when it happened, what it cost, who did it. The strip is the channel's idiom: one bar, one claim, no plate to read as a graphic. It wipes open left to right and the line types into it
  when NOT to use: one of the five entries that carry their own meaning and their own placement — a title (TextTitle), a location (TextPlace), a person (TextName), a passage with one phrase lifted out (TextHighlight), a figure worth counting up (TextCounter). Text that should sit quietly in a corner as bare type rather than in a bar (TextTag)
- TextCounter (anchor types: number/percentage; holds ~4s)
  when to use: the narration lands a single figure worth dwelling on and it should be written onto the shot rather than shown on a card — '$7.9 trillion', '29,000 tanks', '70%'. The number counts up to its value
  when NOT to use: the figure belongs on a graphic with a plate and a rule (AnimatedCounter); two figures being compared (ComparisonSplit) or three and more (BarChart); a number already written out as words, which is just text (TextTitle); a position in a countdown or list ('número cinco', '#3') — it counts nothing, so a count-up reads as a figure the script never states: mark the entry with RankLabel where the menu has it, otherwise as text (TextTag or TextTitle)
- TextHighlight (anchor types: none — pure text allowed; holds ~5s)
  when to use: a sentence or passage on screen where ONE phrase is the point — `mark` names that phrase and the rest of the line drops back behind it
  when NOT to use: the whole line matters equally (TextTitle); several phrases need marking, or the marks should land one after another (HighlightedPassage); the words are a quotation being attributed (QuoteBlock)
- TextName (anchor types: none — pure text allowed; holds ~3.5s)
  when to use: introducing a person by name while the narration continues; `role` is who they were
  when NOT to use: a person's words rather than their name (TextHighlight); the name IS the title card (TextTitle); a lower third with a plate and a rule is wanted (NamePlate)
- TextPlace (anchor types: none — pure text allowed; holds ~3s)
  when to use: the footage already shows a location and the viewer only needs it named — a corner label that does not interrupt the shot
  when NOT to use: the viewer needs to know WHERE the place is rather than what it is called (SatelliteLocate); the place is the subject of the whole shot and deserves the frame (TextTitle)
- TextTag (anchor types: none — pure text allowed; holds ~3.5s)
  when to use: a line of text that none of the role entries fit — a duration, a rank, a count, a caption — with an optional label under it, set as bare type. One of the pack's two general-purpose entries: it does not know what it is saying, so YOU choose where it sits
  when NOT to use: the line is one of the five things that carry their own placement: a title or a figure written as words (TextTitle), a location (TextPlace), a person (TextName), a passage with one phrase lifted out (TextHighlight), a figure worth counting up (TextCounter). Reach for a role entry whenever one fits — it puts the text where that kind of text belongs without being asked. The same line stated in a bar across the frame rather than written into a corner (TextBanner)
- TextTitle (anchor types: none — pure text allowed; holds ~4s)
  when to use: the one line the shot is about — an opening title, the subject being named, or a figure written out as words ('14,2 MILHOES km2'). `sub` is the quiet line under it: a pronunciation, a translation, a date
  when NOT to use: a passage where one phrase matters more than the rest (TextHighlight); labelling where we are while the shot continues (TextPlace); naming a person (TextName)
- Timeline (anchor types: none — pure text allowed; holds ~6.5s)
  when to use: two to six dated events strung in order, where the dates themselves matter — a campaign, an escalation
  when NOT to use: one date (DateStamp); stages with no dates (StepFlow); measurements over time (LineChart); unordered claims (BulletList)
- WaterfallChart (anchor types: comparison; holds ~6.5s)
  when to use: how ONE total was built up or eaten away — an opening figure, the things that moved it, the closing figure. Each column starts where the last one stopped
  when NOT to use: separate quantities compared against each other rather than adding up to something (BarChart); parts of a whole with no sequence (PieChart); a level moving over time (LineChart); stages of a process with no arithmetic (StepFlow)
- WebPageFrame (anchor types: none — pure text allowed; holds ~6s)
  when to use: a web page is the evidence — a filing, an article, a product listing — and the shot has to show both that it is a real page and which part of it matters. `highlight_region` pushes in on that part
  when NOT to use: a post rather than a page, where who said it is the point (SocialPost); a photograph or a document image with no browser around it (FramedExhibit, DocumentCard); a press headline (HeadlineStack)
