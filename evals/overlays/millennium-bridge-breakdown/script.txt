On 10 June 2000, London opened the Millennium Bridge. It closed two days later.

Two thousand people crossed at once. The deck began to sway sideways, up to 70 millimetres.

The engineers had checked vertical loads. Nobody had checked what a walking crowd does to a deck sideways.

The effect is called synchronous lateral excitation. When a bridge sways, people widen their stance to match it, and every corrected step pushes the deck further.

The fix took two years and 5 million pounds: 91 dampers bolted underneath the deck.

Design sway allowed: 5 millimetres. Sway measured on opening day: 70 millimetres.

The bridge was never unsafe. It was just honest about the physics nobody had modelled.
