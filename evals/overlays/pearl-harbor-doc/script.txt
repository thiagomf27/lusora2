On the morning of December 7th, 1941, the United States was still a country at peace. By nightfall it was not.

The first Japanese aircraft reached Pearl Harbor at 7:48 in the morning. The attack lasted 110 minutes. When it ended, eight battleships had been sunk or damaged, 188 aircraft were destroyed, and 2,403 Americans were dead.

Franklin Roosevelt spent that evening dictating a message to Congress. In the draft he crossed out the words "a date which will live in world history" and wrote instead: "a date which will live in infamy."

The next afternoon the Senate voted for war, 82 votes to nothing. The House agreed, 388 to 1.

What followed was an industrial mobilization without precedent. In 1941 American shipyards launched fewer than 100 merchant vessels. By 1943 they were launching more than 1,900 a year.
