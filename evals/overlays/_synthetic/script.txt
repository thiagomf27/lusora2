In 1943 the Soviet Union built twenty-nine thousand tanks. Germany built twelve thousand. The gap was not a matter of courage, and everyone on both sides knew it. It was a matter of factories, and the factories were still standing. Nobody wrote any of it down at the time. The men who kept the counts were told the numbers were a state secret, and most of them died believing it. What survives is a ledger in Chelyabinsk, four hundred pages long, in one clerk's handwriting. Seventy percent of the Soviet tank fleet came off the lines in that single year.
